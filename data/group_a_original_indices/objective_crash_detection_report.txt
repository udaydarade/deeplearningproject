OBJECTIVE CRASH EPISODES AND DETECTION PERFORMANCE
==================================================

This report does not define events from news labels such as "dot-com",
"Lehman", "COVID", or "2022 bear market". Every episode below is produced
from the observed S&P 500 prices and a fixed forward-return rule.

Objective label definition
--------------------------
For each trading date t, calculate the minimum S&P 500 return over the next
15 trading days:

    forward_min_return(t) =
        min_k [(P[t+k] - P[t]) / P[t]],  k = 1,...,15

The threshold is the 2nd percentile of the full-sample distribution:

    theta = -0.1141326196  =  -11.4133%

A date is labeled positive when forward_min_return(t) <= theta. Consecutive
positive dates are collapsed into one episode. This makes the episode start
an objective signal-origin date: from that date, the next 15 trading days
contain an observed drawdown of at least 11.4133%.

The current Group A price sample contains 7,475 prices from 1997-01-03
through 2026-09-21, with 7,460 valid forward-label dates. It contains 150
positive dates collapsed into 31 contiguous episodes.

Important interpretation
------------------------
The label is a forward-looking evaluation target. It is suitable for asking
"did an indicator rise before a large future drawdown?" It is not a claim
that an investor knew the future drawdown on the label date.

For each objective episode, the existing detector diagnostics searched the
five trading observations before through five observations after the label
run. A metric was counted as detected when it first exceeded:

    mean(metric over previous 100 available observations)
      + 1.5 * standard deviation(metric over those observations)

This threshold is fixed from prior observations for each episode and metric.
The reported lead is the calendar-day difference between the episode start
and the first exceedance. A positive lead means the exceedance happened
before the objective label start. Results are aggregated across point-cloud
windows 30, 50, and 100. This is a transparent retrospective diagnostic,
not a fully pre-registered trading strategy.

Detection results across the 31 objective episodes
---------------------------------------------------
The detector outputs are aggregated over windows 30, 50, and 100:

  TDA L1 detected in at least one tested window:       8 / 31 (25.8%)
  rolling variance detected in at least one window:  24 / 31 (77.4%)
  GARCH volatility detected in at least one window:  23 / 31 (74.2%)

These are detection counts, not accuracy counts. A detector can exceed its
baseline during a positive episode without doing so before the episode
starts. It can also exceed the baseline in ordinary periods that are not
labeled positive. A proper precision/recall study would need to count those
negative-period alarms too.

What this objective analysis says
----------------------------------
1. The forward-return rule finds many more objective episodes than the two
   named cases in the original paper. Several episodes occur close together
   because each date is evaluated separately over the next 15 trading days.
   This is expected for a rolling forward-loss label and is not evidence
   that the market experienced 31 independent macroeconomic crises.

2. Rolling variance and GARCH respond to the objective episodes much more
   often than TDA L1 under the current +1.5 standard-deviation diagnostic.
   That is a measured result, not a weakness artificially imposed on TDA.

3. TDA detects some objective episodes, including parts of the 2001–2002,
   2008, 2011, 2020, and 2022 periods, but it misses most objective
   episodes under this operational baseline rule.

4. The objective episode label is generated with a 15-day future horizon.
   Therefore a positive date is defined because a future loss eventually
   occurs; it is not itself the date on which a crash visibly began.
   Any lead-time claim must compare the indicator's first alarm date with
   this objective label start and must be reported separately from the
   future drawdown that created the label.

5. The method therefore detects elevated volatility and some topological
   stress, but the current evidence does not show that the objective
   crashes were completely predictable beforehand. The honest measurable
   statement is that, under this particular fixed label and baseline rule,
   TDA L1 detected 8 of 31 positive episodes, while rolling variance and
   GARCH detected 24 and 23 respectively.
