GIDEA–KATZ REPRODUCTION: WHAT THE VALUES ACTUALLY SHOW
========================================================

Purpose
-------
This document explains the numerical results in
paper_exact_250day_diagnostics.csv. It is deliberately evidence-first:
the paper's method is reproduced as far as the available data and the
paper's stated definitions permit, but the results are not converted into
a stronger prediction claim than the values support.

The most important conclusion is:

    These indicators did not make both historical crashes completely
    predictable. They provide retrospective early-warning evidence for
    some events, especially the 2008 Lehman period, but they do not define
    an operational alarm date and they do not produce the same pattern for
    the 2000 dot-com period in this rerun.

What the paper actually measured
--------------------------------
1. The four daily log-return series (S&P 500, DJIA, NASDAQ, Russell 2000)
   are treated jointly as points in R^4.
2. A Vietoris–Rips filtration is computed for rolling point clouds of
   w=50 trading days. w=100 is a robustness check.
3. H1 persistence landscapes are summarized by L1 and L2 norms.
4. The paper does not say "raise an alarm when L1 exceeds X". There is no
   binary TDA threshold, no classifier, and no forecast date.
5. The paper then computes, in a separate 500-trading-day rolling window
   over the norm series:
       - variance,
       - average low-frequency spectral density,
       - lag-1 autocorrelation.
6. Those statistics are inspected over the 250 trading days before two
   already-known dates: 2000-03-10 and 2008-09-15. The paper uses a
   Mann–Kendall trend statistic to quantify the visual trend.

Therefore "predictable" in the paper means "a retrospective upward trend
was visible before a known event", not "the method generated a validated
real-time signal with known false-alarm and missed-event rates."

Data and reproducibility limits
-------------------------------
The supplied Group A data starts in 1997. The paper's data starts in 1987.
Consequently, the dot-com case has only 205 valid pre-event rows in the
CSV, rather than the full 250 rows requested by the paper's design. The
Lehman case has the full 250 rows.

The downloaded price file is the project's existing Close-price file. The
paper describes adjusted closing values downloaded from Yahoo Finance.
That distinction can change returns and persistence diagrams, especially
around corporate actions.

The paper says "average spectral density at low frequencies" but does not
give a numerical frequency cutoff in the text or source. This rerun uses
the mean of the first five nonzero FFT bins. That is a transparent,
reproducible assumption, not a claim that the exact unpublished cutoff has
been recovered.

Interpretation:
  The TDA norms rise overall and peak shortly before the event, so the
  figure can be read as retrospective evidence of increasing topological
  activity. However, the available interval is shorter than the paper's
  250-day interval, the low-frequency curve is not a clean monotone climb
  into 2000-03-10, and the reported diagnostic trends are sensitive to the
  exact ordering convention used for Kendall tau. This is not sufficient
  to claim a reliable real-time dot-com forecast.

The raw values show the substantive pattern more clearly than the signed
tau labels: variance rises by about 16.8 times, low-frequency power rises
by about 28.9 times, and the TDA L1 norm also rises into late August and
September. This is the strongest retrospective warning pattern in this
rerun. It still does not prove that a live observer could have selected the
Lehman date in advance, because no alarm threshold or false-positive rule
was specified.

Why the current results cannot show "all events were completely predictable"
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. The event dates were supplied in advance. The analysis is therefore
   event-conditioned and retrospective.
2. There is no pre-registered alarm rule. Without a rule, "the curve was
   rising" cannot be scored as a prediction.
3. There is no control sample with a fixed false-alarm calculation in the
   original paper.
4. The dot-com period is truncated by the 1997 start of the supplied data.
5. The low-frequency cutoff is unspecified in the paper and assumed here.
6. The original study contains only two named events, which is inadequate
   for estimating generalization, sensitivity, or false-positive rates.
7. A warning curve can rise during a normal volatile period and then fail
   to precede a crash. That is exactly why a real-time evaluation must use
   rolling out-of-sample thresholds and include non-crash periods.

What can honestly be said
-------------------------
The method detects a change in the joint four-index return geometry and
its persistence-landscape norms become visibly larger around high-stress
periods. In this rerun, the Lehman period shows a clear increase in TDA
norms, rolling variance, and low-frequency power before the event. The
dot-com period shows elevated TDA norms and some upward diagnostic
movement, but not a clean, fully specified, numerically reproducible
forecast signal.

The correct presentation language is:

  "The indicators provide retrospective early-warning evidence, strongest
   for the 2008 crisis, but the paper does not establish that crashes were
   completely predictable. An operational prediction claim requires a
   threshold chosen without looking at the event, a lead-time definition,
   and evaluation across many crash and non-crash periods."

The plots in paper_reproduction_figures/ are intended to show that exact
distinction: the four prices and TDA norms are descriptive trajectories;
the rolling statistics show the paper's diagnostic quantities; and the
ordinary-day bands provide context without pretending to be a validated
alarm threshold.
